The challenge

Growing waste affects our air, water, soil and climate.

Open dumping, uncontrolled burning and poorly managed landfills create pollution and release methane. Waste-to-energy (WtE) is one part of a wider solution: it recovers energy from selected waste that remains after prevention, reuse, recycling and composting.

Why responsible management matters
☁

Cleaner air

Controlled treatment is safer than open burning of mixed waste, which can release smoke and harmful pollutants.

💧

Protected water & soil

Better collection and treatment can reduce leachate from unmanaged waste reaching land and water.

♻

Less methane

Organic waste can be diverted from poorly managed landfills, where it releases a powerful greenhouse gas.

Major technologies

Different waste needs different solutions.

Anaerobic digestion

Microorganisms break down food waste, manure and suitable organic materials without oxygen to produce biogas.

Controlled combustion

Selected residual waste is treated in engineered facilities; recovered heat can generate steam and electricity.

Landfill-gas recovery

Wells and pipes capture some gas from landfills for energy use or safer flaring.

Gasification & pyrolysis

Thermal processes with limited or no oxygen can produce gas, liquid products and char from suitable feedstock.

The key principle

Energy recovery is not the first choice.

The waste hierarchy puts prevention first. Clean paper, metal, glass and recyclable plastics should usually remain materials - not fuel. WtE is most useful for appropriate residual waste after better options have been used.

Do it safely

A plant is sustainable only when its performance proves it.

Separate hazardous and special waste.
Use effective pollution-control equipment.
Monitor emissions and publish results.
Test and safely manage ash and digestate.
Protect workers and involve communities.
Compare costs and impacts with alternatives.
Our shared responsibility

Reduce waste. Segregate at source. Recover value responsibly.

Households, schools, businesses, local authorities and WtE operators all have a role in building a cleaner, more circular future.
